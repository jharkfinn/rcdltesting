# Li et al., "Scaling to Tens of Thousands of Test-Time Iterations with Loop-Native Attention Residuals" (InfiLoop), arXiv 2610.11570v2, 16 Oct 2026

Read end to end on 2026-10-19: main text, appendices A–D, every table and figure caption. The figure images themselves were not inspected.

## What the paper claims

- **Latent overthinking.** In looped transformers with the carry-last rule (h_t = z_t: the block output replaces the state), later iterations overwrite correct intermediate states.
  - Setup: Sudoku-Extreme, TRM (7M parameters), 26 layers per outer update.
  - TRM peaks at 62.2% exact accuracy at 468 executed layers and falls to 58.1% at 1,872.
  - It flips 20.9% of correct cells to wrong at least once during updates 36–72.
  - After solving, its relative state change ‖h_t − h_t−1‖ / ‖h_t−1‖ drops to 3% and then climbs back to 10% per update. It has lost 19.7% of its solutions 36 updates later.
- **The fix, InfiLoop.**
  - Rule: h_t = Σ_i α_t,i z_i, with α_t,i ∝ exp(s_i − λ(t − i)).
  - s_i = qᵀ RMSNorm(z_i) is the content score, with one learned pseudo-query q (in R^d) shared over iterations.
  - λ > 0 is a learned decay, and β = e^−λ.
  - Exact streaming form: N_t = β N_t−1 + e_t z_t, D_t = β D_t−1 + e_t, h_t = N_t / D_t, with e_t = exp(s_t). The memory is O(d) and does not depend on the loop count.
  - Equivalently h_t = h_t−1 + γ_t (z_t − h_t−1), with γ_t = e_t / (β D_t−1 + e_t), between 0 and 1: an adaptive convex step, like a GRU update gate.
  - Cost: d + 1 parameters per recurrence axis. It is applied to both the inner and the outer loop of a TRM-style nested reasoner.
- **Results** (7M parameters):
  - Sudoku-Extreme: 97.9% exact (TRM 74.7% reported, 73.6% retrained; FPRM 94.2%).
  - Maze-Hard: 87.4% (TRM 85.3%, FPRM 87.0%).
  - ARC-AGI-1: 49.1% pass@2 (FPRM 47.5%, TRM 44.6%).
  - ARC-AGI-2: 13.6% pass@2 (TRM 7.8% reported, 4.6% as re-evaluated; FPRM 6.2%).
  - Test-time depth on a fixed Sudoku sample (exact accuracy):

    | Model | 1,872 layers | 3,744 layers | 24,960 layers |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | InfiLoop | 90.9% | 92.4% | 92.74% |
    | FPRM | — | 90.1% | 91.63% |
    | TRM | — | — | 83.65% |

- **Mechanism evidence.**
  - The learned step γ_t is about 0.40 while searching, 0.91 at the update that completes the puzzle, and then returns to 1 − β = 0.56.
  - The content score falls monotonically with the number of wrong cells: median 7.25 for a correct board, −0.58 at 33 wrong cells.
  - After a state perturbation ε from 1e-4 to 0.3 at update 36, InfiLoop keeps 100% of its solved puzzles at update 72. TRM loses 15–20% even without perturbation.
  - Removing the score (q = 0) costs 6–7 points, whether done in training or at test time. Setting β = 0 at test time gives 1.5%.

## Critical reading

- **The headline number does not match the paper's own depth study.**
  - Table 1 gives 97.9% on Sudoku-Extreme.
  - Section 4.4 and Figure 3 report at most 92.74% even at 24,960 layers, and the ablations reach 82.1–89.7%.
  - The text does not say which inference procedure gives 97.9%. Table 1 only says "under their respective inference procedures".
  - This gap is the largest open question in the paper.
- **"Continues to improve beyond 20,000 effective steps" is overstated.**
  - Going from 1,872 to 24,960 layers (13 times the compute) adds 1.8 points (90.9 → 92.74). Most of that comes before 3,744 layers (92.4).
  - The curve has nearly flattened. The abstract's "added depth translates directly into stronger reasoning" goes further than these numbers.
- **The baselines are not matched.**
  - TRM is retrained once with its published recipe.
  - FPRM is the released checkpoint, trained by its own authors, and is not retrained under the same budget.
  - The other rows are numbers reported in other papers under other procedures; URM, for example, reports pass@1.
  - No seeds or error bars are given for any number.
- **The overthinking diagnosis mixes the connection with the training.** It compares TRM and InfiLoop checkpoints that differ in architecture and in what the weights were trained on. The β = 0 test-time ablation (1.5%) shows the InfiLoop weights cannot run carry-last, so it does not isolate the effect of the connection on a fixed network.
- **"Self-verification" is a correlation.** The score tracks board correctness after end-to-end training with deep supervision. The q = 0 ablation shows the score matters (6–7 points). It does not show the model verifies anything.
- **The compute claims count layer evaluations, not runtime.** The authors say so (Appendix A). FPRM's fixed-point solver operations are left out of the accounting.
- **The tasks are constraint-satisfaction and visual-rule reasoning.** There is no knowledge-recall task, so whether the result transfers to fact storage is untested.

## Relevance to our looping test (REPRODUCTION.md §7.5d)

Our loop is a residual update, h ← h + a f(LN(h)) with a = 1, on one tied FFN. It is not carry-last replacement. It still shows the same phenomenon:
- Trained at K = 4, the D = 256 Cos-Bochner layer is at 1.000 at pass 4 and falls to 0.82 at pass 8 and 0.34 at pass 16.
- Its relative step stays 0.18–0.20 per pass.
- TRM's state change after solving is about 0.10 per update, and its accuracy also falls past the trained depth.

The paper's perturbation result also matches our noise probe. With carry-last-like updates the passes amplify a push of the state (our propagated-noise accuracy falls with K). A content-gated convex update is the paper's remedy.

**Test this suggests, cheap on the facts task.** Put the InfiLoop aggregation on our looped FFN: the candidate is z_t = h_t−1 + f(LN(h_t−1)), with h_t = N_t / D_t. Then measure:
1. accuracy at K_test > K_train (overthinking);
2. propagated against end-added noise (clean-up);
3. whether it rescues GeGLU's loss under looping.

If gating rescues GeGLU, Cos-Bochner's advantage under plain looping is mainly stability. If Cos still gains more, the kernel matters beyond stability.
